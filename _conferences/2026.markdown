## Australian System Safety Conference

The Australian System Safety Conference (ASSC) is organised by the Australian Safety Critical Systems Association (aSCSa), a Special Interest Group of the Australian Computer Society.

The theme for the 2026 conference is **System Safety in an Era of Continuously Evolving Capabilities**. This conference seeks to explore the latest research, development, and practical experiences related to the design, implementation, and evaluation of computer systems where failure can have significant consequences. We welcome both academic papers presenting original research and experience reports detailing real-world case studies and lessons learned. Join us to share your insights and contribute to the advancement of this vital area.

### Topics of Interest

Topics of interest include (but are not limited to) systems engineering, safety assurance, human factors, cybersecurity and cyber worthiness, security assurance, reliance, automation, and assurance standards.  

The 2026 ASSC would like to examine and share the latest thinking and state of the art techniques for:
- Providing assurance of systems with evolving capabilities; and for
- Development and maintenance of critical computer systems.

We invite research papers and project experience presentations.

Safety critical areas of interest include:
- Transport 
- Defence and Aviation
- Medicine and Health (medical devices, digital health, etc.)
- AI (in relation to safety)
- Telecommunications
- Energy 
- Security
- Resource and Process Industries
- Emergency Services

### Submission

Delegates have the option of submitting:
1. Research or Industry Papers, refereed by the conference program committee
2. Industry Presentations (not subject to peer review)

Both types of papers are published on the conference website.

- Abstracts should be approximately 250 words.
- For delegates who optionally submit a full paper - papers should be no more than 10 pages.
- A paper template is [available here](/assets/docs/conferences/2026/ASSC%202026%20Template.rtf).
- Paper submissions can be emailed to [secretary@ascsa.org.au](mailto:secretary@ascsa.org.au).

### Important Dates
- Abstract: **30th June 2026** (text, rtf, MS-Word or pdf)
- Submission: **4th September 2026** (rtf, MS-Word or pdf)
- Camera-ready copy: **16th October 2026** (pdf only)

### Best Paper Award
The purpose of this award is to encourage research in the science of software/system engineering or the application of that science for safety and/or mission critical software-intensive systems.

One award is available, consisting of $2000 (Australian). For papers with multiple authors, the award will be given to the lead author.

## Keynotes

### Professor Jill Slay
![Professor Jill Slay](/assets/img/conferences/2026/jill_slay.png){:class="w3-circle w3-right"}
*SmartSat Chair of Cybersecurity*

Professor Jill Slay is currently the Australian SmartSat Co-operative Research Centre Professorial Chair in
Cybersecurity. Her work focuses on the context of supporting development of a holistic national technical agenda in
satellite cybersecurity and resilience with Government, Defence, and Defence Industry. Professor Slay has twenty-five
years of university cross-disciplinary cyber security curriculum and research development after establishing herself as
a cyber security academic drawing on engineering, ICT, and systems security. In a technical context, Professor Slay was
ranked as being in the top 2% of scientists in the world in Artificial Intelligence, ICT Networking and
Telecommunications sub-field, since she was an early adopter of AI and Machine Learning in cyber security she applies
these to satellite security. Professor Slay is a member of the Australian Research Council (ARC) College of Experts
and Vice Chair of the IEEE P3349 - Space System Cybersecurity Working Group, which has developed technical standards
in space systems cybersecurity.

### Dr Kelvin Ross
![Kelvin Ross](/assets/img/conferences/2024/kelvin_ross.png){:class="w3-circle w3-right"}
*Chairman, KJR  | Director, IntelliHQ | Adjunct Assoc Professor, Institute for Integrated & Intelligent Systems*

Dr Kelvin Ross is an entrepreneur, technologist and researcher with over 25 years’ experience in software engineering,
safety-critical systems and enterprise IT. He began his career in safety-critical software engineering in defence,
working on FA-18 airborne radar systems, before completing a PhD in safety-critical systems engineering and spending
several years consulting in defence and transportation systems. He then moved into the commercial sector, founding KJR,
a mid-tier IT consultancy specialising in software testing and assurance across e-health, public administration,
finance, insurance, retail and telecommunications. Kelvin has contributed to several boards and national committees,
including IntelliHQ, a healthcare AI innovation NFP, Chair of the Qld AI Hub, ACS national technical committee for
AI Safety. He is an Associate Adjunct Professor at Griffith University’s Institute for Intelligent and Integrated
Systems. Over the past two years he has been applying agentic AI tools hands-on across coding, test automation
and delivery, and speaks regularly on AI safety, governance and assurance.
